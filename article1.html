Personal Evangelism: Facing Fear, Loving Souls, and Fulfilling Our Calling By Gary — Deacon of Personal Evangelism Personal evangelism is simple to describe but challenging to live. At its core, it means bringing lost souls to Christ one person at a time and helping them remain faithful. As I said in my lesson, “Simply put, it means bringing lost souls to Christ… one person at a time… and helping them to remain faithful.” But there’s far more to it than that. I’ve never been comfortable speaking in front of people. I’ve struggled with fear, stuttering, shaking hands, and even moments where my mind goes blank. I’ve faced emotional battles, including a breakdown that nearly took my life. Yet here I am — not because I’m strong, but because God is. And because I cannot ask others to face their fears unless I face mine first. If someone like me — short, bald, nervous, and scared — can stand up and speak about Jesus, then anyone can talk to others about Him. Our Obligation to Share the Gospel Every Christian has already “signed up” for evangelism. When we obeyed the gospel, we accepted the responsibility to help others find Christ. As I said in the lesson, “ALL of us have an obligation to tell others about Jesus… you already signed up when you became a Christian.” Jesus made this clear in the Great Commission: “Go therefore and make disciples of all the nations…” — Matthew 28:19 People rarely walk up and ask us to teach them the truth. We must go to them. Resources That Help Us Teach I shared several tools that can help Christians — including young Christians — learn how to teach others: • Apologetics Press children’s books (simple, short, and powerful) • Back to the Bible • Believe the Bible • Fishers of Men • Open Bible Study • Searching for Truth • Jule Miller Filmstrips • WBS Connect Program • House to House / Heart to Heart These tools are helpful, but they only work if we can get someone to sit down and study with us. As Rob Whitacre says, “We have to earn the right to study with someone.” People don’t care how much we know until they know how much we care. That’s why personal evangelism must be personal. Making Evangelism Personal Not everyone will teach a Bible study, but everyone can help make evangelism personal: • Smile • Shake hands • Say hello • Send a card • Pray • Cook a meal • Donate materials • Show up for worship As I said in the lesson, “There’s something for everyone to do… even our little kids can do that.” When people know we care about them, they are far more open to studying the Bible — and far more likely to remain faithful after obeying the gospel. The Importance of Faithful Attendance Evangelism is weakened when we ourselves are not faithful. It’s hard to bring lost souls to Christ if we’re not present. I shared examples of: • Young families who show up despite challenges • Those who “play injured” spiritually or physically • The elderly who remain faithful through hardship My own father is a powerful example. Even when my mother was gravely ill, he still attended worship because he believes God always comes first. That kind of faithfulness strengthens the whole church. Jesus taught the principle clearly: “First remove the plank from your own eye…” — Matthew 7:3–5 We cannot help others until we first examine ourselves. Evangelism begins with our own faithfulness. Why God Uses Us God could have chosen angels to preach the gospel. He could have chosen miracles. But instead, He chose us. As I quoted in the lesson: “We have the treasure of the Good News in clay jars… so the power belongs to God and not to us.” — 2 Corinthians 4:7 We are imperfect, fragile, and broken — and that’s exactly why God uses us. When we teach others, the glory goes to Him. Motivation: Fear, Courage, and Love Fear can stop us from speaking. But fear can also motivate us. Revelation 21:8 lists “the cowardly” first among those who will face judgment. That’s sobering. As I said, “Somehow I don’t think fear is going to work as an excuse.” But courage is not the absence of fear — it is facing fear and doing what is right anyway. Joshua 1:9 reminds us: “Be strong and of good courage… for the Lord your God is with you.” And above all, love must motivate us. True love is doing what is spiritually best for someone, no matter the cost. If we truly love God and love souls, we will speak. The Reality of Spiritual Warfare We are in a spiritual war. Ephesians 6:11–12 makes that clear. In my lesson, I described a scene from the movie Heaven’s War where souls were falling into hell one after another. It’s a terrifying reminder that eternity is real and time is short. Peter warns us: “Your adversary the devil walks about like a roaring lion…” — 1 Peter 5:8 If we love people, we must warn them. Evangelism Begins With Us The purpose of evangelism is to save people from sin — but it must start with us. We must examine our own lives, repent where needed, and put God first. As Scripture teaches: • Judgment begins with the house of God (1 Peter 4:17) • We must reconcile with our brother (Matthew 5:23–24) • We are not our own — we were bought with a price (1 Corinthians 6:19–20) If we are not faithful, the whole body suffers. As I said in the lesson, “If part of our body hurts, the whole body suffers.” Today Is the Day We cannot wait. We cannot delay. We cannot assume tomorrow will come. Paul wrote: “Now is the day of salvation.” — 2 Corinthians 6:2 If something stands between you and God, fix it today. If someone you love is lost, reach out today. Conclusion I love the church deeply. You have blessed my life, strengthened me, and helped save me. I am willing to do anything to help you, and I believe you love me too. Let’s work together — not just to bring souls to Christ, but to keep them in Christ. This is personal evangelism. This is our calling. This is our purpose. Amen.
